# The note layout — the strongest concept in Series 1

Source: Series 1 doc §5.

## What it is
On a piano, notes run left to right in pitch order. On a tenor pan they do not.
Notes are arranged in a **cycle of fourths and fifths** — each note sits next to
notes a fourth or a fifth away, wrapping around the drum in the pattern that
gives the "spider web" pan its name.

## Why it was done that way
- **Harmonic relationship.** Notes a fourth or fifth apart are the notes most
  often played together. The layout puts chord tones under the hand rather than
  scattered across the surface.
- **Sympathetic resonance.** Harmonically related notes placed adjacent reinforce
  rather than fight each other when struck.
- **Tuning.** A layout built on a consistent intervallic formula is easier to
  tune accurately and sounds cleaner than an arbitrary arrangement.

## Why it's excellent short-form
- A genuine "you didn't know that" — most viewers, including many musicians,
  assume a pan is laid out in pitch order.
- The reveal is visual, immediate, and needs only a top-down shot of a pan and an
  overlay.
- No archive material, no Chrome Factory footage, no permissions.

This is the strongest concept in the series. It should be an early episode and is
a strong candidate for episode 1 alongside the headline claim.
