# The heroes

Source: Series 1 doc §4. Each has a one-line angle for short-form.

## Winston "Spree" Simon (1930–1976)
From Laventille, Port of Spain. Played with Tokyo. Credited with the **ping pong**
— the first pan able to carry a complete melody, initially ~8 distinct pitches.
Performed calypso and classical, locally and abroad, showing what the instrument
could do when it was still widely dismissed. Member of TASPO. Some consider him
the inventor of the steelpan.
**Angle:** the man who proved a beaten oil drum could play a tune.

## Ellie Mannette (1927–2018)
Born in Sans Souci; played with Invaders. Widely called "the father of the
modern steel drum." Two innovations every pan still carries:
- **Concave sinking** — sinking the playing surface inward instead of leaving it
  convex, fitting far more notes on one drum.
- **Rubber-wrapped sticks** — softening the attack for a rounder, more musical tone.
Also associated with firing the metal to improve its acoustic properties. Member
of TASPO. Spent decades teaching pan-making in the US; died in West Virginia, 2018.
**Angle:** every pan played anywhere today uses his two ideas.

## Anthony Williams (1931–2021) — "Muffman"
From St. James; led North Stars. Invented the **"spider web" pan** — the
fourths-and-fifths layout that became, and remains, the standard for the tenor /
lead pan. Member of TASPO. Chaconia Medal (Gold); Order of the Republic of
Trinidad and Tobago.
**Angle:** he decided where the notes go — and nearly every lead pan in the world
still follows his decision.

## Bertie Marshall
From Laventille. Associated with the development of the **double tenor** and with
early work on amplifying the pan; known for tuning innovations involving heat
treatment.
**Angle:** the man who tried to make pan louder, and changed the instrument doing it.

## Rudolph Charles
Leader of Desperadoes; central figure in the steelband movement. Associated with
expanding the bass range and with organisational leadership that professionalised
pan sides.
**Angle:** the organiser — the reason pan became orchestras rather than street bands.

## TASPO — the 1951 twelve
Assembled from the best players across ~70 Trinidad steelbands and sent to the
Festival of Britain in 1951. Members drawn from bands including Invaders,
Crossfire, Syncopators, Southern Symphony, Hill 60, Free French, North Stars,
Rising Sun, Casablanca, Tokyo, Sun Valley.
**Angle:** rival bands from rival neighbourhoods, put in one orchestra and sent to
London. The strongest single narrative in pan history, and underused.

## The contested credit — DO NOT RESOLVE ON SCREEN
Sources genuinely disagree on who first used the 55-gallon oil drum. Some credit
Mannette; others credit Anthony Williams. The disagreement is real and unresolved.
**Rule:** do not pick a side and state it as fact. Make the disagreement the
content — it is more interesting than either answer, and handling it honestly
signals you know the subject. A confident wrong attribution is the fastest way to
lose the pan audience.
